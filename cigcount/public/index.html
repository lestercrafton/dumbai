Cigarette Count Sheet

For a gas station in Fayette County, Tennessee. Take a picture of the cigarette wall. Check the count. Match it to your deliveries and register. Get the sheet.

Why am I counting all of this?

Tennessee checks your cigarette sales without coming to the store first.

Your wholesaler tells the state what they sold you. The state compares that to the sales tax you reported. That program is called the Retail Accountability Program. If the numbers look short, the state can send a tax bill based on its own math.

Your count is your proof. The math is always the same:

Packs you started with + packs delivered − packs the register sold = packs that should be on the shelf.

If the shelf has less, packs are missing — sold off the register, stolen, broken, or counted wrong. If it has more, a delivery or sale was written down wrong. This page does that math for you and shows its work.

Every pack for sale in Tennessee must also carry a Tennessee tax stamp. The wholesaler normally stamps them and the stamp tax — 62¢ a pack — is built into what you paid. Do not sell unstamped packs. Keep this sheet, your invoices, and your register reports together.

Before you start

Fayette County is usually 9.25% total. Check your TNTAP return if yours is different.

Your work saves on this phone as you go. It does not leave this phone except the photo you send for counting.

Step 1 — Photograph the cigarettes

  1. Stand straight in front of one shelf section. Get close enough to read the pack names.
  2. Take one picture. Then move to the next section. Add the back stock and any open cartons too.
  3. Open every open carton and count the packs inside. A sealed carton counts as 10 packs. An open carton does not.

You can also pick photos you already took. Blurry picture? Retake it — a bad photo makes a bad count.

No photos yet. Add a photo, or use “Add a brand by hand” inside a photo box after you add one. You can also do a full hand count in Step 2.

Step 2 — What came in and what sold

Use the time since your last count. “Started with” is that last count. “Delivered” is everything the wholesaler brought since then. “Register sold” is from your register report for the same days.

Counted packs come from Step 1. If you counted by hand with no photo, type them in the Counted column.

Step 3 — Your sheet

Give the printed sheet to your bookkeeper with the invoices and register report for the same dates. Keep a copy with the photos.

How do I know it did not make a mistake?

  1. The picture counter can be wrong. Packs hide behind packs. That is why it never gets the last word.
  2. Under each photo, look at every brand row. Fix packs and cartons with the − and + buttons. Rows marked Needs a look are the ones the counter was unsure about.
  3. Tap the photo to drop a numbered dot on a pack it missed. Pick the brand first. Tap a dot again to take it off.
  4. Only check “I checked every row against the shelf” when you really did. Until then, the sheet says DRAFT — NOT CHECKED in red. A checked sheet shows who checked it and when, on the sheet itself.
  5. In Step 3, read “What this means.” A difference is not proof anyone stole. Recount that brand first.

This page helps you count and do the matching math. It is not tax advice. Tennessee cigarette stamp tax is 62¢ per pack of 20 (Tenn. Code § 67-4-1004), normally paid by your wholesaler through the stamp. Wholesalers report their sales to you under the Retail Accountability Program (Tenn. Code § 67-6-410), which is why the state can compare deliveries to your sales tax return. If the state sends you a notice, take this sheet, your invoices, and your register reports to your bookkeeper or tax person promptly — notices have appeal deadlines.
